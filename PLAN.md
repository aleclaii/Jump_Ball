## The App

**Jump Ball** settles everyday decisions, like Panda Express or Subway for lunch, by making the choices play a short sports match: basketball, MMA, soccer penalties, or football, picked at random. The winner is the decision. Odds are either fair or weighted by "ability": Alex's idea is that a weight works like the gap between a 15 ppg NBA starter and a 25 ppg all-star. That ability affects the whole game, not just a single coin flip. It's for Alex, mostly on their phone, and it's aimed at the "Best Excuse to Avoid Studying for Finals" award.

The app is a TypeScript + Vite project (see `README.md` for how to run it and how the code is organized). The original single-file prototype is kept in `prototype/jump-ball.html` and at https://claude.ai/artifact/9aCH7nF29NCGh4oUjvFpM1.

- 2026-10-13: **Trading game design**, chosen by Alex over four rounds of questions (Alex is into economics and asked to be asked more so they could learn). Replaces Claude's first sketch, which Alex set aside to start from scratch.

  - Prices: hidden true value (fundamentals + noise, which Alex is "really interested in") plus a mood. Alex picked a hybrid: neutral market makers that charge the bid-ask spread, and a bull/bear crowd with herding (bubbles and crashes) from behavioral finance. Traders have tiny price impact.
  - Information: same public news for both, plus paid consultants for private noisy research. Noise falls with a pricier firm and with the trader's Research and Network ("consultants work better when the client knows what they want"); Network also lowers fees. Scheduled earnings and Fed rate decisions.
  - Shorting allowed with a high cap ("closer to real life but without the actual unlimited loss").
  - Winner: higher Sharpe ratio (risk-adjusted return); Alex wants to learn how it's evaluated.
  - Stats (7, budget 35): Research, Network, Macro, Risk, Discipline, Quant, Execution. Plus a free Bear-to-Bull slider and a Contrarian switch outside the budget.

  - Screen: three panels, all with true value and the mood gauge shown to viewers. The portfolio race is big; the combined stock chart (all stocks on one graph, which is fine because "the user is just watching") and the trading floor are small. Big on the left with the two small ones stacked on the right; on a phone, big on top and the two small side by side below. Claude's choice: the stock chart plots % change since day 1 so different-priced stocks share a scale.
- 2026-10-13: **Trading balance choices (Claude's, from simulations).** Full Markowitz sizing (using how stocks move together) scored a worse Sharpe than simple sizing (0.94 vs 1.17 over 3000 games) because it magnifies errors in expected returns, the same finding as DeMiguel et al. (2009), so every trader uses simple risk-scaled sizing. Risk Control is therefore about avoidable mistakes: gut-feel bet sizes and "pet" positions, which add swings with no expected return. Consultants are hired before earnings (where private research matters most) and judged against a typical bet, not the whole portfolio. Claude also picked: dollar prices with stocks starting $40–160 (Alex asked for dollars), the name "Risk Control" (higher = more careful), and default styles (Joe slightly bullish, Sam slightly bearish and contrarian).

## Problems

- 2026-10-13: **Chess balance and realism took three passes.** First, Tactics and Calculation won 86–90% at 10 vs. 1 while Endgame did nothing: a 1 in Tactics hung pieces constantly, and in a 20-move game the "endgame" was rarely reached. Fixed by softening the weak end, making the final 40% of the move cap count as the endgame, and making Composure blunders likelier. Then openings looked random ("1.h4 e6 2.h5"), because weakness was modeled as noise added to every move; now weaker players choose among the top few moves, and the scoring knows basic opening principles.
- 2026-10-13: **Trading stats barely mattered at first** (most were 50–60% at 10 vs 1, Risk Control even lost). Knockout experiments found the causes one by one: valuation bets lost money even with perfect information because positions were so small that the "skip small trades" rule froze them (fixed with a band relative to the position); mispricings closed too slowly to beat trading costs (now about 5 days); consultants cost more than they earned (traders now judge a report against a typical bet); position caps hid the cost of gut-feel sizing (added pet positions); the crowd's mood drowned out rival links (added a shared sector mood and toned mood down). Rival stocks now move together 0.44 vs 0.19 for unrelated ones.
- 2026-10-13: **Mini 4X snowballed.** At first Economy won 100% at 10 vs. 1 and Military 96%: a small edge compounded into bigger cities, more production, more captures. Fixed with smaller stat effects, wider battle luck, and a "rally" bonus that makes an empire with fewer cities defend harder. Now every stat is 70–77%.
- 2026-10-13: **Two similar team colors made the 4X map unreadable** (Taco Bell and Subway were both green). Added the contrasting stand-in color for the second side.
- 2026-10-13: **The win-chance estimate froze the page for chess** (each chess game takes ~0.2 s to simulate, too long to slice up). Moved the practice games into a Web Worker (a background thread): zero blocked moments over 50 ms during a 40-game estimate.
- 2026-10-13: **A chess notation bug**: when no list of legal moves was passed in, a move compared itself to a fresh copy of itself and added a needless square ("Qd8h4#" for "Qh4#"). Fixed by comparing squares; regression tests added.
- 2026-10-13: **A flaky test**: "a higher rating wins more, but not every time" played 1,000 full basketball games at 90 vs. 55, which wins 99.8%, so it sometimes saw zero upsets and failed by chance. It now uses a realistic 82 vs. 70 quick game (about 72%), and the suite passed 15 runs in a row.
- 2026-10-13: **Couldn't switch to a saved option from the name box** (Alex: the dropdown arrow appeared but offered nothing to pick). Cause: the browser's built-in suggestion list (`datalist`) only suggests names matching what's already typed, and the box already held the current name. Fixed with a custom picker that always lists every saved option; a jsdom test now checks the list appears when the box is tapped.

- 2026-10-07: **MMA was far too predictable at first**: 90% of even fights ended in knockouts, and a 25-point rating gap won 99.6% of the time, because many small exchanges average out. Fixed by giving fighters more health, softening how much rating matters per exchange, and adding rare big "rocked" shots that take 30% of a health bar. Now even fights are 50/50, a 12-point gap wins about 76%, and knockouts happen in about 1 in 5 quick fights.
